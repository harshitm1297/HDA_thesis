# Phase 4 pathway interpretation and patient heterogeneity

## Analytical status

Phase 4 passes its computational checks and produces a reproducible systems-biology interpretation of the Phase 3 no-imputation branch. Milestone M4 is nevertheless **conditional on completion of Phase 2 and full M3 closure**. The pathway results inherit every limitation of the current upstream branch; they are not a substitute for the missing preprocessing benchmark.

Reactome release 86, released on 7 September 2023, is frozen locally by SHA-256. It is the only external knowledge resource used in this phase. It contributes pathway membership, not new measurements, clinical annotations or imputed values. Release 86 keeps the analysis inside the project's research cutoff and provides a curated human pathway vocabulary ([Reactome release 86](https://www.reactome.org/about/news/230-version-86-released); [Gillespie et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkac958)).

## Two complementary pathway views

The first view asks whether proteins belonging to a Reactome pathway accumulate toward the positive or negative end of the complete Phase 3 moderated-statistic ranking. Ambiguous compound identifiers are excluded. If a gene symbol occurs more than once, the source row with the largest absolute moderated statistic is retained under a deterministic audit rule. This gives 3,430 ranked symbols, of which 2,680 occur in Reactome. Pathways with 10–300 measured symbols are tested, yielding 783 ranked tests. The weighted running-sum statistic, gene-set permutations, sign-specific normalization and leading-edge extraction implement a dependency-light, pre-ranked GSEA-like procedure based on the framework of [Subramanian et al. (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0506580102). One thousand permutations imply finite p-value resolution; the smallest possible empirical p-value is 1/1,001.

The second view retains only protein rows observed in all 42 pairs and carrying an unambiguous gene symbol. Each patient's tumour-minus-matched-non-tumour difference is divided by a robust feature scale. It is deliberately **not** centred on the observed patient median, because zero must remain the null value of no paired change. A pathway score is the mean of its available standardized changes. Twenty thousand whole-patient sign flips test whether the cohort-average pathway score differs from zero while preserving the covariance among proteins within each patient. There are 749 pathways with at least five fully observed proteins. This is a transparent paired score, not an ssGSEA score and not an independent validation cohort.

Benjamini–Hochberg correction is applied separately to the 783 ranked-pathway tests and the 749 paired-score tests ([Benjamini and Hochberg, 1995](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1995.tb02031.x)). At q≤0.05, 328 ranked pathways and 566 patient-score pathways pass. There are 271 pathways that pass both views with the same direction. The two views use different representations and null models, but both derive from the same 42 patients; their agreement is therefore internal robustness evidence, not biological replication. Reactome pathways overlap and are nested, so the 271 rows are also not 271 independent discoveries. Pathway enrichment is used to organize signal rather than inflate a list of mechanistic claims, consistent with the cautions reviewed by [Khatri, Sirota and Butte (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrg3175).

## Dominant biological organization

The convergent positive side contains RNA processing and protein-production machinery. Processing of capped intron-containing pre-mRNA has normalized enrichment score (NES) 3.297, ranked q=0.00559, mean paired score 0.831 and paired-score q=0.000161. mRNA splicing has NES 3.261 and mean paired score 0.865 at the same respective q-value floors. Translation has NES 2.253, ranked q=0.00559, mean paired score 0.438 and paired-score q=0.000426. These results state that measured proteins assigned to these pathways tend collectively toward higher tumour-minus-non-tumour values; they do not establish pathway activity, transcriptional flux or causal activation.

Immune and antigen-handling modules also appear on the positive side. Antigen processing–cross presentation has NES 2.151, ranked q=0.00559, mean paired score 0.352 and paired-score q=0.000161. Cytokine signalling in the immune system has NES 2.236, ranked q=0.00559, mean paired score 0.257 and paired-score q=0.000297. Viral-disease-labelled Reactome sets appear because they reuse interferon, nuclear transport and host-response proteins; their names do not imply that these tumours have a viral infection. Interpretation must be based on the measured member proteins and shared host machinery rather than the literal disease label.

The convergent negative side is dominated by mitochondrial energy metabolism and several extracellular or plasma-associated systems. The citric-acid-cycle/respiratory-electron-transport pathway has NES −2.835, ranked q=0.00559, mean paired score −1.165 and paired-score q=0.000161. Respiratory electron transport has NES −2.869 and mean paired score −1.242 at the same q-value floors. Collagen degradation has NES −2.082 and mean paired score −0.723; extracellular-matrix degradation has NES −1.974 and mean paired score −0.629; complement cascade has NES −2.129 and mean paired score −0.550. Each has ranked q=0.00559 and paired-score q=0.000161. Hemostasis is less extreme but directionally concordant (NES −1.440, ranked q=0.0140, mean paired score −0.323, paired-score q=0.000161).

These signs describe the supplied tissue extracts and cannot distinguish altered tumour-cell regulation from stromal content, immune composition, vascular/plasma contribution, sampling depth or other composition effects. They also do not prove that a whole pathway is uniformly altered: the ranked output records leading-edge members and the network edge table exposes the exact feature-to-pathway links used.

## Patient-change geometry

Heterogeneity analysis uses no imputation. Of the 8,071 source rows, 1,350 are finite in all 42 patient differences. Rows with zero robust variability are removed, the 500 most variable remaining rows are median/MAD scaled, robust z-scores are clipped at ±5, and PCA is fitted to the patient-change matrix. This feature restriction is unsupervised and is used only to describe variation between paired response profiles.

PC1 explains 41.41% of the robust-scaled variance, with a 200-bootstrap median of 42.20% and a percentile interval of 29.83–52.42%. PC2 explains 16.13% (bootstrap median 17.42%, interval 11.72–24.25%). The first four components explain 70.72%, so four PCs enter the prespecified clustering stability analysis. Large absolute PC1 loadings include CYC1, UQCRB, ALDOA, NDUFV1, NDUFS3, ATP5F1B, SDHB, OGDH, SUCLA2, DLD and MT-CO2; PCA signs are arbitrary, so only their joint contribution to the axis is meaningful.

Consensus clustering evaluates k=2–5 with 200 resamples containing 80% of patients. Acceptance requires PAC≤0.10, mean within-cluster consensus≥0.80, silhouette≥0.25 and at least five patients per cluster. No k passes. The least ambiguous exploratory solution is k=2, with clusters of 12 and 30 patients, PAC 0.261, within-cluster consensus 0.934 and silhouette 0.372. Because PAC fails by a wide margin, these labels are retained only as computational response patterns and the `accepted_cluster` field is empty. They are not oral-cancer subtypes. This conservative use of consensus stability follows the resampling principle introduced by [Monti et al. (2003)](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1023949509487) and the PAC-based cautions of [Şenbabaoğlu, Michailidis and Li (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep06207).

## Deliverables and boundaries

`integrated_pathway_evidence.csv` contains one row per tested Reactome pathway with both evidence views, direction agreement and a display priority. `reactome_ranked_enrichment.csv` retains the ranked statistic, permutation result, measured membership and leading edge. `reactome_patient_score_tests.csv` contains paired-score tests, while `patient_pathway_scores.csv` contains the 42 patient-level pathway profiles. `feature_pathway_network_edges.csv` makes leading-edge feature membership auditable. PCA loadings, bootstrap intervals, cluster stability, patient coordinates and consensus matrices are all saved separately.

The phase does not establish causality, clinical subtypes, biomarkers, diagnosis, prognosis or external validity. It has no batch labels, acquisition order, tumour purity, histology, HPV status, anatomical subsite, demographic covariates or outcomes. Existing literature cannot manufacture these variables. Phase 5 may use the present pathway and protein representations only inside leakage-safe patient-grouped validation; it must not treat pathway selection on all 42 patients as a prevalidated feature filter.
