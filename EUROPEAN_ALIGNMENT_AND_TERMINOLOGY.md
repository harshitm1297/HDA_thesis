# European alignment and terminology note

> Data-only status: this document supplies terminology, metadata design and claim boundaries. It does not imply that unavailable clinical, pathology, ethics or governance fields will be recovered. Under the current operating position, those fields remain explicitly unknown and their consequences are tracked in `DATA_ONLY_LIMITATIONS_AND_ASSUMPTIONS.md`.

> Scope note: this is a current Europe-facing context document and therefore contains post-2023 regulatory and terminology developments. It does not justify or change the analytical methods. The project's scientific methods and AI design are restricted to evidence available through 31 December 2023 in `THESIS_EXTENSION_DATA_SCIENCE_AI_PLAN_2023.md` and `RESEARCH_EVIDENCE_BASE.md`.

## Status and scope

This note records how the oral-cancer proteomics project is aligned with European clinical terminology, research-data practice, data protection, artificial-intelligence governance, and translational expectations as they stand on 1 October 2026. It does not assert that the cohort was recruited in Europe, that European law governs the original collection, or that an exploratory analysis is a regulated medical device. Instead, it makes the analysis legible to European collaborators, reviewers, repositories, ethics bodies, and future validation sites while retaining population-specific information from the source cohort.

The practical consequence is that European alignment is implemented inside the data model and claim language. Anatomical site, morphology, staging edition, specimen relationship, consent basis, data-access conditions, and intended use are recorded explicitly. They are not reconstructed from a gene-expression pattern, inferred from a filename, or replaced with a generic European assumption.

## Disease language and cohort definition

The project will use **oral cancer** only as the broad label supported by the surviving report. It will use **oral cavity squamous cell carcinoma (OCSCC)** only for cases whose pathology and anatomical site establish that entity. The 2024 fifth edition of the WHO Classification of Head and Neck Tumours treats “oral cavity and mobile tongue” separately from the oropharynx and lists oral squamous cell carcinoma as a defined entity. The base of tongue belongs to the oropharynx rather than the oral cavity. This distinction matters because anatomical site, HPV association, staging, prognosis, and treatment pathways differ. The cohort must therefore not be relabelled wholesale as OCSCC, HNSCC, or HPV-related disease until the original pathology and site fields are recovered. [WHO/IARC Head and Neck Tumours, 5th edition](https://publications.iarc.who.int/Book-And-Report-Series/Who-Classification-Of-Tumours/Head-And-Neck-Tumours-2024) and the [WHO classification structure](https://whobluebooks.iarc.who.int/structures/head-and-neck-tumours/) provide the current terminology.

European clinical literature often uses **SCCHN** or **HNSCC** for a family of squamous carcinomas, but the EHNS–ESMO–ESTRO guideline retains site-specific assessment for oral cavity, oropharynx, hypopharynx, and larynx. For an oral-cavity case, pathology fields should therefore include exact subsite, morphology, grade, depth of invasion, margins, perineural and lymphovascular invasion, lymph-node findings, extranodal extension, and the staging system used. These fields are clinically relevant covariates and sources of biological heterogeneity; they are not merely administrative labels. [EHNS–ESMO–ESTRO Clinical Practice Guideline](https://www.annalsofoncology.org/article/S0923-7534%2820%2939949-X/fulltext).

The neutral companion specimen will be called **matched non-tumour tissue**, not normal, healthy, control, adjacent normal, or NAT unless the collection record establishes those descriptions. Distance from the tumour, mucosal subsite, histological review, dysplasia, inflammation, oral potentially malignant disorder, and tumour-cell contamination must be recovered where possible. A visually uninvolved margin can still reflect field cancerisation, inflammation, stromal composition, or premalignant change. This is why the paired contrast estimates a tumour-versus-sampled-non-tumour tissue difference within affected patients; it is not a cancer-versus-healthy-population comparison.

If stage is recovered, the manifest will retain the original `cTNM`, `pTNM`, stage group, source document, assessment date, and **TNM edition**. UICC recommends TNM 9 from 1 January 2026, whereas a cohort analysed or collected earlier may have been staged under TNM 8. The project will not translate an historical stage group into TNM 9 without the component information needed for a valid conversion. [UICC TNM resources](https://www.uicc.org/resources/tnm/publications-resources).

## European relevance without population overreach

The study can contribute to European oral-cancer research because the biological pathways and candidate proteins can be compared across cohorts, but direct transportability is not assumed. European Cancer Information System reporting separates lip/oral cavity, oropharynx, nasopharynx, hypopharynx, salivary gland, and laryngeal cancers rather than pooling all head-and-neck sites. The 2024 estimates were updated across EU-27, EFTA, and other European countries in 2026, reinforcing the need for site-specific comparisons. [ECIS database description](https://ecis.jrc.ec.europa.eu/database-description) and [2026 ECIS update](https://ecis.jrc.ec.europa.eu/news/updated-incidence-and-mortality-estimates-2024-are-now-available-eu-27-efta-and-other-european).

European populations and the likely source population may differ in tobacco type, smokeless-tobacco and areca/betel-quid exposure, alcohol patterns, oral submucous fibrosis, HPV distribution, diet, socioeconomic context, access to diagnosis, stage at presentation, treatment, and ancestry. The manifest therefore retains locally important exposures such as smokeless tobacco and betel quid while adding European-comparison fields rather than replacing one vocabulary with the other. External validation must report site, morphology, exposure distribution, geography, recruitment setting, stage, treatment status, and analytical platform before a result is called replicated. A candidate that is directionally consistent across distinct settings is stronger evidence; a candidate that differs may identify effect modification rather than simple failure.

Europe’s Beating Cancer Plan and the European Cancer Inequalities Registry emphasise prevention, early detection, high-quality diagnosis and treatment, and inequalities across countries and socioeconomic groups. Those priorities support collecting sex, age, geography, deprivation or education where lawfully available, exposure history, and access-to-care variables. They do not convert this paired surgical-tissue dataset into a screening study. The European Commission’s own 2024 inequalities review described population biomarker screening as an area requiring evidence on effectiveness and safety. Any future screening claim needs an intended-use cohort containing asymptomatic people and clinically relevant benign and premalignant comparators in the intended specimen type. [Europe’s Beating Cancer Plan](https://health.ec.europa.eu/non-communicable-diseases/cancer_en) and [European cancer inequalities report](https://health.ec.europa.eu/publications/report-beating-cancer-inequalities-eu-spotlight-cancer-prevention-and-early-detection_en).

## Research data, consent, and access

For European collaboration, the manifest must distinguish a project sample code from any re-identification key. Patient IDs in analytical files should be pseudonymous, access-controlled, and stored separately from direct identifiers. Under the GDPR, health data are a special category of personal data; scientific research requires an applicable Article 6 basis, an Article 9 condition, and Article 89 safeguards such as data minimisation and pseudonymisation where the purpose can be achieved that way. Pseudonymised data remain personal data when a party can reasonably re-identify the person. Consent, ethics approval, lawful basis, controller/processor roles, retention, permitted secondary use, international transfer, and withdrawal handling must therefore be recovered from institutional records rather than presumed from the existence of an Excel file. [GDPR, Regulation (EU) 2016/679](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj) and the [EDPB pseudonymisation guidance](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2025-01/edpb_guidelines_202501_pseudonymisation_en.pdf) set the governing concepts.

The European Health Data Space Regulation entered into force in 2025 and will apply progressively. It expressly includes proteomic and other human molecular data among categories for secondary use, alongside research cohorts and biobank data. It does not by itself supply the present project with consent, an immediate data permit, or a retrospective lawful basis. Its practical value now is to shape a future-compatible catalogue record: dataset title and description, responsible holder, purpose, population, geographical and temporal coverage, data categories, coding systems, quality and utility information, access conditions, legal restrictions, update history, and linkage possibilities. The project will mark which fields are verified, inferred, unavailable, or not applicable. [EHDS Regulation (EU) 2025/327](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/327/oj) and [Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2098 on dataset-description metadata](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2026/2098/oj).

The source workbook must not be placed in a public repository until identifiability, consent, ethics, data-controller approval, and access conditions have been reviewed. If sharing is permitted, raw spectra and complete search outputs should be deposited through ProteomeXchange, normally via PRIDE at EMBL-EBI, using controlled access or an appropriate access route if necessary. The analysis code, data dictionary, non-disclosive aggregate QC, software environment, and accession metadata can be separated from participant-level data. PRIDE supports mass-spectrometry evidence and community formats such as mzML, mzIdentML, and mzTab and is an ELIXIR Core Data Resource. [EMBL-EBI proteomics resources](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/about/teams/proteomics-metabolomics/) and [ProteomeXchange submission guidance](https://www.proteomexchange.org/docs/guidelines_px.pdf).

## Statistical and AI lifecycle

The present classifier is an exploratory **tissue-state classification model**, not an artificial-intelligence diagnostic device. The patient is the resampling unit; feature filtering, transformation, normalisation, imputation, scaling, selection, tuning, calibration, and threshold selection remain inside training folds. Performance will be reported with uncertainty, calibration, and stability, using TRIPOD+AI for transparent reporting and PROBAST+AI for risk-of-bias review. Site, sex, age, exposure and geography should be examined as transportability variables when adequate metadata and sample sizes exist, because pooled performance can conceal clinically important European inequalities.

The EU AI Act does not apply to an AI system specifically developed and put into service solely for scientific research and development, but that exclusion should not be treated as a permanent label for a model that later acquires a medical intended purpose. If software is intended to provide diagnostic or treatment information and falls under the MDR or IVDR, the AI Act’s high-risk provisions can apply when the system is a medical device or safety component subject to third-party conformity assessment. The 2025 joint AI Board/MDCG guidance explains this interaction. The project will therefore maintain an explicit intended-use statement, versioned data provenance, training and test separation, foreseeable limitations, human oversight assumptions, and change history from the research stage onward. [EU AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj) and [AIB 2025-1/MDCG 2025-6](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/b78a17d7-e3cd-4943-851d-e02a2f22bbb4_en?filename=mdcg_2025-6_en.pdf).

A protein panel measured in human specimens for diagnosis or risk stratification may enter the IVDR pathway, while software with a medical purpose may qualify as medical device software. Clinical translation would then require an intended-purpose statement, analytical validity, scientific validity, clinical performance, risk management, usability, post-market planning, and, where applicable, conformity assessment. The current work supplies discovery evidence only and must not use `validated`, `diagnostic`, `clinical-grade`, `CE-mark ready`, or `screening test` as shorthand. [IVDR, Regulation (EU) 2017/746](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2017/746/oj) and [MDCG 2019-11 rev.1](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/b45335c5-1679-4c71-a91c-fc7a4d37f12b_en?filename=mdcg_2019_11_en.pdf).

## Repository implementation

The following fields define a European-compatible schema, but under the data-only position unavailable project values remain `unknown`. Public reference annotations may be populated with a source and version; project-specific clinical or governance values must not be guessed:

- exact oral subsite and coding system, including whether mobile tongue or base of tongue;
- pathology entity and WHO classification edition;
- clinical and pathological TNM components, stage group, TNM edition, and assessment date;
- specimen type, sampling relationship, distance from tumour, histological review, dysplasia, inflammation, purity, and margin status;
- age at collection, sex recorded at birth where available, relevant gender information only when justified, country/region, ancestry or ethnicity only where lawfully and scientifically appropriate, and socioeconomic variables where available;
- smoking, smoked product, pack-years, smokeless tobacco, areca/betel quid, alcohol, HPV method and result, and oral potentially malignant disorders;
- treatment before collection, centre, collection date, preservation, preparation batch, instrument batch, run order, and technical controls;
- ethics committee, approval identifier, consent scope, lawful basis, controller, permitted secondary use, retention, transfer restrictions, and public-access status;
- data and software versions, checksums, database releases, ontology versions, transformations, exclusions, and responsible analyst.

The terms `unknown`, `not_applicable`, and `withheld` are intentionally distinct. `Unknown` means the project has not established the value; `not_applicable` means the field does not apply; `withheld` means the value exists but is not released in the current analytical record. This distinction prevents missing governance or clinical data from being mistaken for a negative observation.

## Immediate effect on Phases 0 and 1

Phase 0 now includes a European-compatible claim boundary, pathology vocabulary, staging-version requirement, ethics and data-governance inventory, and regulatory transition boundary. Its remaining gaps are not filled with European defaults; they are made explicit and assigned a recovery route.

Phase 1 remains structurally valid because it preserves source identifiers, sample pairing, row order, and unknown metadata. Its manifest should next be extended with the European-compatible fields above, without fabricating values. A second, versioned annotation table should map exact anatomical and pathology terminology only after source records are obtained. Current HGNC and UniProt annotations may be added as new fields with database version and retrieval date, but they must never overwrite the original Spectronaut-derived row label.
